In a nutshell

Understanding what
an object affords.

Open-vocabulary 3D affordance grounding identifies the parts of an object that support an action. A central challenge is transferring interaction knowledge from 2D images to 3D objects, especially when their shapes or functions were not seen during training.

QueryMe learns from multimodal evidence. It reconstructs a human–object interaction image in 3D, focuses on relevant spatial regions, and uses learnable queries to retrieve evidence from interaction, text, and object representations. The resulting point-wise predictions locate functional regions on the target object.

On PIADv2, QueryMe improves unseen-affordance AUC by 4.19 points over GREAT.

More precise functional regions

Qualitative comparisons on microwave opening and bag lifting show the predicted regions alongside ground truth and GREAT.

Resilience to noisy geometry

QueryMe retains approximately 69 AUC when spatial perturbations affect 85% of target-object points in the paper's noise experiment.

Scope & limitations +

These results concern object-level affordance grounding on PIADv2. Monocular reconstruction errors, occlusion, and background clutter remain challenges. Fine-grained unseen affordances can be difficult to localize. On the unseen-object split, GREAT has a lower MAE (0.109) than QueryMe (0.118), while QueryMe leads on AUC, aIoU, and SIM.
