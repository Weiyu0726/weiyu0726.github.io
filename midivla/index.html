01 / THE INSIGHT

Learning what changes.
Separating what stays.

A video transition contains both action and appearance. MiDiVLA learns to separate them across scales, so a compact set of tokens can supervise robot actions.

How is the noise applied? ＋

Both frames are encoded into three feature scales. For the selected scale, blend its continuous features with Gaussian noise before codebook lookup. At 0%, keep the original features. At 100%, replace them with noise. Then quantize and reconstruct.

The noise draw is fixed across the 21 strengths. The other two scales retain their clean token indices and contributions. These are recorded reconstructions for one image pair; the animated diagrams illustrate the intervention.

02 / THE METHOD

Separate the dynamics.
Keep the context.

Three training objectives shape a compact,
compositional latent action space.

01

Reconstruct

Multi-scale tokens predict the future observation. The hierarchy retains both transition dynamics and visual detail.

Sx + {r1, r2, r3} → Ŝx+t
02

Concentrate

Corrupt the coarsest scale and require a return to the initial state. This encourages a first-scale action bottleneck.

Sx + {r̃1, r2, r3} → Sx
03

Compose

Add adjacent first-scale action embeddings to predict a two-step transition, promoting reusable dynamics.

ax,t + ax+t,t → ãx,2t
From latent actions to executable control.

Infer first-scale tokens from Internet video → pretrain the VLA to predict these tokens from image and language → fine-tune on a small set of robot trajectories.
